# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to build something good, and say what
you mean without making it personal.

## What this means here

- **Critique the design, not the designer.** "This claims enforcement it does not
  have" is the review this project wants. "You clearly don't understand security"
  is not the same sentence.
- **Be accurate about what you know.** Say what you observed and what you assumed.
  Overclaiming is the failure mode this template is built to resist, and that
  applies to conversation about it too.
- **Respect the honest limits.** Documented limitations are not bait. Pointing out
  that behavioral controls are not a sandbox is agreeing with the README.
- **No harassment**, in any form: personal attacks, sustained disruption,
  demeaning comments about who someone is, unwelcome attention, or publishing
  someone's private information.
- **No real people in examples.** Fixtures, issues and discussions use fictional
  communities and fictional people. This is a privacy tool; it should behave like
  one in its own repository.

## Scope

Issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any space where someone is
representing this project.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainer through the repository's contact path. Security issues go
through `SECURITY.md` instead — do not open a public issue for one.

Reports are handled privately. What happens next is proportionate: a clarification,
a request to change behavior, removal of a comment, or a block. Someone acting in
good faith who got it wrong is not the same as someone acting in bad faith, and
they will not be treated the same.
